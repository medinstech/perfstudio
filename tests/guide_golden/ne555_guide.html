Soldering guide · 32×22 FR4 perfboard at 2.54 mm pitch · 22 steps, 39 checks · PerfStudio {VERSION}

Phase 0 — Preparation

Cut the board, mark hole A1, and get the iron and the parts ready.

On the bench

  • Soldering iron with a fine tip, set to 350 °C
  • 60/40 or lead-free solder, 0.7–1.0 mm
  • Flux — a pen or a small pot. Solder traces are not reliably makeable without it
  • Side cutters and small pliers
  • A multimeter with a continuity buzzer (every checkpoint below uses it)
  • Hookup wire, AWG 24 in black, blue, orange, red, white, yellow — about 0.35 m in total
  • Wire strippers
  • Tinned copper wire for the trace spines, 0.6 mm — about 51 mm (component lead offcuts do the job just as well)

Iron

350 °C, no more than 3 s on any one pad. FR-4 tolerates heat well; the limit here is the component, not the board.

The board

Cut it to 32 × 22 holes (81.3 × 55.9 mm) and mark hole A1 in the top-left corner on the COMPONENT side. Every address below is counted from it, so if it is marked wrong, everything else is.

Phase 1 — Lowest profile

Anything that lies flat: axial resistors and diodes. These go first because a board with a tall part on it will not sit flat on the bench, and a part that cannot be pressed down solders at an angle.

Phase 2 — IC sockets

IC sockets. The ICs themselves go in at the very end -- heat and static are the two things that kill them, and both are avoidable by fitting them last.

Phase 4 — Medium bodies

Medium bodies: electrolytics, TO-220 packages, crystals.

Phase 5 — Tall and mechanical

Tall and mechanical: connectors, terminals, potentiometers, switches, relays.

Phase 6 — Solder side: traces and bare wire

Turn the board over. Solder traces and bare wire, worked one net at a time so each is finished and checked before the next crosses it.

Check before moving on

Phase 7 — Long insulated wires

Insulated wire, which may cross anything already on the solder side.

Check before moving on

Phase 8 — Closing up

Fit the ICs, run the final checks, and power up under current limit.

Check before moving on
